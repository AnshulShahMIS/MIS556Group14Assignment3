SUPPORT COPILOT HUMAN LABELING STUDY

Purpose
Measure intent and urgency accuracy plus escalation precision, recall and F1
against independent human judgments. Do not treat model outputs or historical
brand replies as ground truth.

Assignments
Rater A: rater_A_assignment.json, 60 tweets.
Rater B: rater_B_assignment.json, 60 tweets.
Both assignments include the same 20 tweets, four per brand. Together they
cover 100 unique tweets, 20 per brand. Both raters work independently before
discussing the shared cases. Do not copy the other rater's labels.

How to label
1. Open the app's Evaluation lab and expand the human-labeling section.
2. Upload your assignment JSON and select Load label file.
3. Enter intent, urgency, needs_human, a short escalation reason, and a rater
   identifier in annotator. Use Rater A or Rater B if you prefer anonymity.
4. Save and Export human labels. Save the downloaded JSON outside the browser.
   Reloading or closing the session loses access to its workspace in the UI.
5. Compare both completed files with tools/compare_human_labels.py.
6. Discuss disagreements only after independent work. Record a consensus label
   and the reason for changing it in the consensus draft. Never erase raw files.

Intent rubric: select the primary help requested
refund: refunds, duplicate charges, billing or payment disputes.
delay: late/missing packages, flight delays, missing/delayed baggage.
account: login, passwords, profile/access problems, suspected account compromise.
bug: an app or service feature fails, crashes or behaves incorrectly.
praise: a compliment with no primary request for help.
other: remaining topics, spam/off-topic, or an issue that fits no category.
If there are multiple issues, label the main requested action and explain any
ambiguity. Do not create new categories halfway through the study.

Urgency rubric
high: immediate safety/security risk, active compromise, or an explicit
      time-critical deadline requiring prompt attention.
med: a current unresolved service problem with no evidence of immediate danger.
low: general information, praise, or non-time-sensitive feedback.
Negative wording or anger alone does not establish high urgency.

Human-review rubric
true: legal/safety/account-compromise risk, an actual private-account decision,
      or insufficient policy evidence to handle the issue safely.
false: applicable public FAQ steps, a safe clarification, routine praise, or
       clearly irrelevant spam that can be ignored without a reply.
Use policy_reference.json for the same limited current-policy context as the
app. Do not assume a refund entitlement, account facts, or VIP status. TWCS does
not provide verified VIP metadata. A customer's claim alone does not establish
VIP status. Assess the need for a human, not whether you like the wording.

Adjudication and reporting
Report overlap size, paired completed labels, raw agreement and Cohen's kappa
for each field. Kappa is undefined when expected agreement is 1. Missing labels
must stay missing; do not convert them into incorrect predictions or zero
accuracy. Use the adjudicated 100-case file for final triage metrics. Keep the
frozen system unchanged during test annotation. If you change the system,
record a new version and a new evaluation rather than selecting favorable
test cases.

Reply-quality validation
After the updated benchmark, sample replies across all five brands. At least
two raters should score a shared subset without seeing system names or model
judge scores. Score helpfulness, factual support and tone from 1 to 5 using the
customer issue and official policy notes. The historical brand reply is only
one observed answer. Keep response quality separate from triage labels.

Status
These files contain NO human ground-truth labels. The system's rule-based
functional tests and model-judged benchmark are different forms of evidence.
